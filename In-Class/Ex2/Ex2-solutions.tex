\vspace{0.6em}

\noindent\textbf{Summaries.}
\[
n=10,\quad
\bar{x}=52.5,\quad
s=\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{9}\sum(x_i-\bar{x})^2}\approx 3.028.
\]

\noindent\textbf{Critical value.}
One-sided 95\% lower bound $\Rightarrow$ $\alpha=0.05$, df $=n-1=9$:
\[
t_{0.05}(9)=1.833.
\]

\noindent\textbf{Lower bound.}
\[
L
=\bar{x}-t_{0.05}(9)\,\dfrac{s}{\sqrt{n}}
=52.5-1.833\times\dfrac{3.028}{\sqrt{10}}
\approx 52.5-1.755
\approx 50.745.
\]
Interval:
\[
[\,50.745,\ \infty\,).
\]

\noindent\textbf{Claim $\mu\ge 50$.}
The entire lower CI lies above 50 ($L>50$), so at the 95\% level the data support the advertising claim.
(Equivalently: a level-$\alpha=0.05$ test of $H_0:\mu\le 50$ vs $H_1:\mu>50$ rejects $H_0$.)

\vspace{0.45em}
\noindent\textit{Common trap.}
Using $t_{0.025}(9)=2.262$ (two-sided) gives a lower $L$ and the wrong critical value for a one-sided bound.
